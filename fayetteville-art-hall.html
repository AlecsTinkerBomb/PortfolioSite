Final Work

Fayetteville art hall

Rooted in the structural logic of the large-span hangar precedent and shaped by the spatial experimentation of the "expand" concept, the art hall translates rigid-frame thinking into a bold, angular form that stands in sharp contrast to the winding path leading to it. That same sense of expansion drives the interior experience—carefully placed light openings shape distinct atmospheres throughout, from soft overhead washes to framed landscape views, all within a structure designed around indirect natural light. The building moves beyond a simple gallery, connecting the wooded Ramble path of Fayetteville, local gathering spaces, and art exhibition into one seamless public experience.

Part 2

Roof Transformation

Building on the large-span hangar precedent, this project explores how a single idea "expand" can transform structure and space. Emerging from a series of nine design experiments, the concept drives a spatial journey from a tight, intimate entry to a soaring open interior, creating a compelling sense of release while staying rooted in the logic of the studied hangar precident.

Roof Precedent

This project examines a large-span hangar's rigid frame structural system alongside envelope strategies for natural ventilation and solar gain mitigation. The accompanying analyses establish this precedent study as the semester's foundational framework.
